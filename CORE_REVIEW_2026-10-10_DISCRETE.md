# Discrete counting and exact probability — technical evidence (2026-10-10)

Scope: substantive bilingual chapter between programming semantics and complexity analysis in the Amazon SDE II and Computer Science core tracks. MIT 6.042J and Stanford CS109 are curricular references; Python itertools/Fraction are source-verified executable contracts.

Proofs: (i) inclusion–exclusion counts each element once; (ii) cardinality of powersets follows independent include/exclude decisions; (iii) binomial coefficients follow counting ordered selections divided by k! and Pascal's partition; (iv) collisions use complement of all-distinct outcomes with independent uniform replacement; (v) expected distinct categories use indicators and linearity; (vi) Bayes uses partition into true/false condition and positive/negative observation. These are separate mathematical arguments rather than claims derived only from tests.

Verification: tests/test_discrete_counting_probability_contracts.py extracts and executes Python examples in both languages. Counting functions are checked against explicit itertools permutation/combination enumeration (n<=8, including k>n), set unions against membership masks (all pairs up to n=5), exact collision probabilities and mean distinct categories against full Cartesian outcome enumeration (days 1..5, people 0..6), Bayes against separately enumerated weighted-state outcomes on a rational grid, and conditional probability against finite cardinality counts. Invalid inputs, undefined zero-probability conditions and Python bool-as-int pitfalls are rejected.

Limits: assumes distinguishable finite objects, explicit equal weights only when stated, independent uniform draws for collisions, and exact rational arithmetic. Not empirical probability estimation, randomness-quality analysis, cryptographic entropy, generalized statistics, complete combinatorics, benchmark complexity or formal verification. Exhaustive tests establish agreement on the checked finite domain, not an independent theorem for every input.
